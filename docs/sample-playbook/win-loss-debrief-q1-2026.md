# Win/Loss Debrief Notes — Q1 2026

**Document type:** Debrief
**Owner:** Sales Enablement
**Last reviewed:** 2026-04-05

This document summarizes recurring themes from Q1 2026 win/loss
interviews, conducted by Sales Enablement with clients after a
quotation reached APPROVED or REJECTED status.

## Themes from won deals

**Fast turnaround on revisions.** Several clients specifically called
out how quickly reps could revise and resend a quotation after a
scoping call, compared to their previous vendor. This came up
independently in four separate interviews with mid-market clients
(50–200 employees).

**Clarity of line-item breakdown.** Clients in the professional
services segment repeatedly said a detailed line-item breakdown
(rather than a single bundled project fee) helped them get internal
budget approval faster, since they could show finance exactly what
each cost covered.

**Named point of contact through approval.** Deals where the same rep
stayed engaged through the client's internal approval process (rather
than handing off to an account manager after signature) had a
noticeably higher rate of expansion within the first two quarters.

## Themes from lost deals

**Price anchoring too high on first quote.** In three lost healthcare
deals this quarter, the client cited "sticker shock" from the initial
quotation before any discount was applied. Feedback from Revenue
Operations: reps should have a pricing conversation before sending a
first formal quotation in unfamiliar segments, rather than leading
with full list price and expecting to negotiate down later.

**Missing multi-currency support.** Two lost deals in the EMEA region
cited the lack of multi-currency line items as a blocker, consistent
with the gap noted in the QuoteWise battlecard. Both prospects went
with QuoteWise specifically for this reason.

**Slow internal approval turnaround.** One notable lost deal (a
$120,000 public sector contract) was lost after our internal approval
for a requested 22% discount took nine business days, during which the
prospect's budget window closed. This is the primary case study behind
the current push to tighten SLA on regional-director-level approvals to
2 business days.

## Segment-specific patterns

Public sector deals showed the longest sales cycles overall (average
94 days from DRAFT to APPROVED) but also the highest close rate once a
quotation was formally sent, at 61%. This suggests the bottleneck for
public sector is pre-quotation qualification and budget-cycle timing,
not the quotation or approval process itself.

Healthcare deals under $50,000 closed at a notably higher rate (58%)
than healthcare deals over $50,000 (34%), reinforcing the tighter
discount discipline described in the discount approval policy for
larger healthcare deals — larger healthcare deals appear to face more
internal scrutiny on the client side regardless of our pricing.
