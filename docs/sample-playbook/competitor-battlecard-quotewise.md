# Competitor Battlecard: QuoteWise

**Document type:** Battlecard
**Owner:** Product Marketing
**Last reviewed:** 2026-03-01

## Who they are

QuoteWise is our most commonly encountered competitor in mid-market deals
($20k–$150k ACV). They position themselves as an "all-in-one" quoting and
e-signature platform and are frequently bundled into broader CRM deals
because they integrate natively with a popular CRM's marketplace.

## Where we win

**Approval workflow flexibility.** QuoteWise supports only a single linear
approval chain per quotation. QuoteIQ supports role-based approval with
full status history, which matters a lot to clients with more than one
layer of sales management. If a prospect mentions they have both regional
directors and a VP sign-off step, lead with this.

**Speed of quote creation.** QuoteWise requires quotations to be built from
a pre-defined template with locked line-item structures. Reps consistently
report QuoteIQ is faster for one-off or heavily customized quotes because
line items are fully freeform.

**Transparent audit trail.** QuoteWise's status history is only visible to
admins, not to the reps who created the quote. QuoteIQ shows the full
status history to the quotation's owner, which reduces "why was this
rejected" support tickets significantly.

## Where they win

**Native e-signature.** QuoteWise has built-in e-signature; QuoteIQ does
not (as of this document, e-signature is not yet on the roadmap — do not
promise a delivery date to a prospect). If e-signature is a hard
requirement, be upfront that QuoteIQ would need to pair with a separate
tool like DocuSign.

**CRM marketplace integration.** QuoteWise has a one-click install into
the CRM prospects are already using. If a prospect's primary objection is
integration friction, acknowledge this directly rather than
overpromising an integration timeline.

**Multi-currency support.** QuoteWise supports line items priced in
different currencies within a single quotation. QuoteIQ currently
assumes single-currency quotations. For international deals, calculate
values in the client's primary currency manually before quote creation.

## Common objections and responses

**"QuoteWise is cheaper."** QuoteWise's list pricing is lower per seat,
but their approval workflow limitations often force customers to
purchase their higher tier to unlock multi-step approvals — at which
point total cost is comparable. Ask the prospect how many approval
steps their deals typically require before comparing price directly.

**"We already use QuoteWise's CRM."** This is a real switching-cost
objection, not a feature gap. Don't argue features here — ask what's
prompting them to look at alternatives in the first place (usually it's
an approval-workflow or audit-trail pain point), and lead with whichever
of our strengths above maps to that pain.

**"Your product doesn't have e-signature."** Acknowledge directly. Most
clients who raise this are already pairing DocuSign with their current
tool anyway; ask if that's the case for them before treating it as a
blocker.

## Win/loss notes from the field

Several closed-won deals against QuoteWise in Q1 2026 cited the visible
status history and role-based approvals as the deciding factor,
particularly among clients with more than 20 sales reps. Deals lost to
QuoteWise in the same period were concentrated among clients under 10
employees, where e-signature and low upfront price mattered more than
workflow flexibility.
